How to Make Collage Animation on Mobile: My Full Process

I create all my collage animations entirely on a phone — no desktop, no tablet. Here is my real workflow from the first idea to the finished film, using Alight Motion and CapCut.

People often assume that serious animation requires a powerful computer and expensive software. My experience is different. Everything I make — including a 10-minute animated film with full character dialogues — was made on mobile, using two free apps.

The workflow I am about to describe is not a shortcut. It is a real production process that produces real results. It is just entirely mobile.

The two apps I use

APP 01 — ALIGHT MOTION

Animation

Alight Motion is where I build and animate everything — characters, scenes, effects. It handles keyframe animation, parent layers, blend modes, distortion, glow and frame-by-frame work. This is where the animation actually happens.

APP 02 — CAPCUT

Assembly and editing

CapCut is where I put everything together. After animating each scene separately in Alight Motion, I import them into CapCut in the right order, add transitions between scenes, layer in sound effects and music. CapCut does not animate — it edits. That distinction matters.

Two apps, one phone. That is the entire studio.

My process step by step

STEP 01 — IDEA

What do I want to show?

Most of the time clients give me creative freedom — they say what they need but let me decide how to do it. So the first thing I do is find the idea. Who are the characters? What is the story? What feeling should the viewer have at the end? I think about this before touching any app.

STEP 02 — SCENES

Planning the structure

Once I have the idea I break it into scenes. How many? What happens in each one? What order do they go in? This is a simple plan — sometimes just notes, sometimes rough sketches. It saves a lot of time later because I know exactly what I need to build.

STEP 03 — CHARACTERS

Building the cast

I create characters from separate pieces — a head, a body, arms, legs, a mouth. Each piece is its own layer in Alight Motion. For collage animation this means cutting real photos or illustrations into parts. The character does not exist as one image — it is an assembly of movable pieces.

The mouth is the most complex part. If a character speaks, I need every mouth position — every vowel, every consonant — drawn or cut separately. This is frame-by-frame lip sync, and it is slow, careful work.

STEP 04 — BACKGROUNDS

Building the world

Backgrounds come from different places depending on the project. Sometimes I find images online. Sometimes I photograph something myself — a texture, a surface, a sky. Sometimes I generate a background with AI tools and then paint over it or combine it with other elements.

Usually one project uses a mix of all three. That mix is part of what gives collage animation its distinctive texture — it feels assembled from the real world rather than drawn from scratch.

STEP 05 — ANIMATION IN ALIGHT MOTION

Bringing it to life

I animate scene by scene. Each scene is its own Alight Motion project. I place the background, add the character pieces, then set keyframes for movement — position, scale, rotation. I use parent layers to connect body parts so they move together naturally. I add effects — glow for cosmic scenes, distortion to make characters breathe, star fields for space backgrounds.

When the scene is done I export it as a video file and save it with a clear name so the order stays obvious.

STEP 06 — ASSEMBLY IN CAPCUT

Putting it all together

Once all scenes are animated and exported, I open CapCut and import them in order. Here I add transitions between scenes, adjust timing, layer in sound effects and choose the music. CapCut is fast and intuitive for this kind of work — it is not an animation tool, but as a mobile editor for assembling finished scenes it does exactly what I need.

The final export comes out of CapCut as a finished film.

The phone is not a limitation. It is just where the work happens.

I have been asked many times whether mobile animation is "real" animation. The answer is in the work. What matters is not the device — it is the idea, the craft and the time you put in.

Questions & Answers

FAQ

Can you make collage animation on a phone?

Yes. I create all my collage animations entirely on mobile using two apps — Alight Motion for animating each scene and CapCut for final assembly, transitions, sound effects and music. No desktop software, no tablet required.

What apps do you need for collage animation on mobile?

I use two apps: Alight Motion for building and animating scenes — cutting character pieces, keyframe animation, effects and lip sync. Then CapCut for assembling scenes in order, adding transitions, sound effects and music. Each app does what it does best.

How do you find images for collage animation?

I use a mix of sources — images found online, photos I take myself, backgrounds generated with AI tools, and elements I draw by hand. Usually one project combines several of these. The mix is part of what gives collage animation its distinctive texture.

How long does it take to make a collage animation?

It depends entirely on complexity. A short 15-second scene can take a few hours. A 10-minute film with character dialogues and multiple scenes took months of work. The most time-consuming part is frame-by-frame lip sync — every mouth position drawn individually.
